# Data Classification

Northstar Digital classifies information based on the sensitivity of the data and the impact of unauthorized disclosure. The company uses three broad categories: public, internal, and confidential. Some projects add an additional label for restricted client data when the client requires more formal handling.

## Classification guide

| Level | Typical examples | Handling expectation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public | Marketing copy, published case studies, public blog posts | Can be shared broadly |
| Internal | Internal notes, planning documents, draft proposals | Share within the company and approved partners |
| Confidential | Source code, internal business data, project plans | Limit to the project team and approved stakeholders |

## Handling rules

Confidential data should be stored in approved repositories or knowledge systems and should not be copied into personal notes, public channels, or open AI tools. When in doubt, the safest approach is to treat the content as confidential until a more precise classification can be confirmed.

## Historical note

This document is archived because the company is currently reviewing a more detailed classification model for client-specific content. Until that revision is approved, teams should use the current definitions and escalate unclear cases to the security team.
